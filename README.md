# CPPA vs. GDPR — Comparative Privacy Analysis

A working paper comparing consent mechanisms across Canadian and EU privacy regimes, and what those differences mean for digital interface design.

---

## 🎯 Objective

Compare how Canada's CPPA (Consumer Privacy Protection Act) and the EU's GDPR handle **lawful basis**, **proportionality**, and **consent** — then translate the legal comparison into practical guidance for interface designers.

## 🔬 Approach

- Compared **lawful basis** requirements across both regimes
- Analyzed **proportionality** — how much data collection is justifiable
- Dissected **consent mechanisms** — what counts as valid consent under each framework
- Assessed implications for **digital interfaces**: consent language, defaults, and task timing

## 📦 Key Deliverable

A **lightweight evaluation checklist** linking privacy requirements to concrete interface decisions:

- ✅ Consent language — is it clear, specific, and unambiguous?
- ✅ Defaults — are privacy-protective defaults in place?
- ✅ Task timing — is consent requested at the right moment in the flow?

## 💡 Why it matters

Privacy regulation is only as good as its implementation in the interface. This paper bridges the gap between legal requirements and the design decisions that determine whether users can actually exercise their privacy rights.
